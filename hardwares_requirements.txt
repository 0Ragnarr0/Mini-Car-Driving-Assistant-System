
## Steering System

| Hardware | What it does | What's lacking if missing |
|---|---|---|
| **Steering servo (1×)** | Turns the front wheels left/right; takes a single steering command from the controller. | No steering at all — car can only go straight. One servo is enough (mechanical Ackermann). |
| **Ackermann steering linkage** (knuckles, tie rod, ball links) | Makes the inside wheel turn sharper than the outside so all wheels follow the same turn center — no tire scrub. | Tires scrub and fight in turns; inaccurate turning radius; bicycle-model control math no longer matches reality. |
| **Front uprights/knuckles with kingpin pivots** | The pivot points the front wheels rotate on. | Wheels can't steer; no place to mount front wheels. |

> **Decision made:** single servo + mechanical Ackermann (simpler, more reliable, fits our RL control). Easiest build: use a **1/10 RC car front end** and 3D-print only the chassis adapter plate.

Search: Thingiverse / Printables for "Ackermann steering," "RC car steering knuckle," or "1/10 RC front upright."
## Wheels & Drivetrain

| Hardware | What it does | What's lacking if missing |

| **4× wheels** (1/10 RC, 12 mm hex, rubber tires + foam inserts, ~60–65 mm) | Contact with ground; rubber gives consistent grip. | Hard/mismatched wheels slip → real behavior won't match the simulator (sim-to-real fails). |
| **Front bearing hubs (2×)** | Let the un-driven front wheels spin freely while steering. | Front wheels bind or drag; steering and rolling are inaccurate. |
| **Rear driven hubs (2×)** | Couple the rear wheels to the motor for propulsion. | No drive to the wheels — car can't move. |
| **Drive motor + ESC/motor driver** | Provides forward/reverse propulsion; ESC controls speed. | No movement; no reverse (needed for parking/backing out). |
| **Differential (if rear-driven)** | Lets rear wheels spin at different speeds in turns. | Rear tires scrub in turns; jerky cornering. |

## Perception & Compute

| Hardware | What it does | What's lacking if missing |

| **Camera (forward-facing)** | Feeds images to YOLOP for lane detection + object detection. | No lane-keeping, no obstacle/lane awareness — the whole perception pipeline is blind. |
| **Onboard compute** (e.g. Jetson Nano/Orin or Raspberry Pi + accelerator) | Runs YOLOP perception and the TD3 policy in real time. | Can't run the neural networks on-car; no autonomy without a tethered PC. |
| **(Optional) distance sensors** (ultrasonic / ToF / small LiDAR) | Backup obstacle detection for stop/avoid/reverse, especially close range and blind spots. | Relies on camera alone; weaker close-range and low-light obstacle safety. |

## Power & Electronics

| Hardware | What it does | What's lacking if missing |

| **Battery (LiPo) + power distribution** | Powers motor, servo, compute, sensors. | Nothing runs. Compute and motor often need **separate/regulated supplies** so motor noise doesn't crash the computer. |
| **Microcontroller** (e.g. Arduino/ESP32) *(optional but common)* | Bridges compute to servo/ESC (PWM), reads sensors. | Compute board may not drive servo/ESC cleanly; harder real-time motor/servo timing. |
| **Chassis / 3D-printed frame** | Holds everything together at correct geometry (wheelbase, track width). | Misaligned geometry breaks Ackermann and odometry; parts shift under vibration. |

## Priority order (if building incrementally)

1. **Chassis + steering (servo, Ackermann) + drivetrain (motor, wheels, hubs)** → car physically drives and steers.
2. **Power system** → reliable, separated power for motor vs. compute.
3. **Compute + camera** → run YOLOP perception.
4. **Deploy TD3 policy** → autonomous control.
5. **(Optional) distance sensors** → extra safety margin for stop/avoid/reverse.
